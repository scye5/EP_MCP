# ROSAT and eROSITA data inventory for EP candidates

Reviewed 22 November 2026. Start with the EP candidate’s position, localization radius and observation date. A catalogue answers which sources were detected nearby. A historical observation can also constrain a candidate when the catalogue has no match.

| Collection | What is available | How it informs an EP candidate |
|---|---|---|
| ROSAT All-Sky Survey (RASS) | PSPC survey scans and processed survey fields in RASSMASTER. The 2RXS catalogue contains about 135,000 detections in 0.1–2.4 keV, with positions, count rates, hardness ratios, quality flags and, where available, spectral or variability measurements. Legacy bright and faint catalogues use earlier processing. | Search 2RXS for a historical source. Inspect quality flags in crowded or diffuse regions. A catalogue count rate needs a spectral assumption before comparison with EP energy flux. [2RXS catalogue](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/588/A103) · [RASSMASTER](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/rosat/rasspublic.html) |
| ROSAT pointed observations | ROSMASTER indexes PSPC and HRI observations by position, time, instrument and exposure. Public processed datasets can contain event lists, images, backgrounds, ancillary files and processing summaries. Availability varies by instrument and sequence. | A pointed exposure can constrain a position outside a known source list. Check the candidate against the actual field and exposure map. Archive coordinates generally describe the pointing centre. [ROSMASTER](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/rosat/rospublic.html) · [HEASARC archive layout](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/rosat/archive_access.html) |
| eROSITA-DE Early Data Release | Calibration and performance-verification fields, including eFEDS. These targeted and survey-like observations have their own footprints and release products. | Check overlap separately when investigating a candidate in one of these fields. [Early Data Release](https://erosita.mpe.mpg.de/edr/) |
| eROSITA-DE DR1 (eRASS1) | First survey pass, December 2019 to June 2020. Main, supplementary, hard and cluster catalogues. The German release has 2,447 overlapping tiles, including partially covered boundary tiles. Products include calibrated events, images, exposure and background maps, sensitivity maps, area curves and selected source products such as spectra and light curves. | Use the main catalogue for the 0.2–2.3 keV baseline, the hard catalogue for 2.3–5 keV, and the maps or upper-limit service to check a specific position. Exposure varies across and within tiles. [DR1 products](https://erosita.mpe.mpg.de/dr1/AllSkySurveyData_dr1/) · [Catalogue interfaces](https://erosita.mpe.mpg.de/erodat/apis/) |
| eROSITA-DE DR2 (eRASS:3) | Main and hard catalogues from three scans combined, December 2019 to June 2021, plus positional upper limits. The public release covers the western Galactic hemisphere. Tile event lists, images, exposure maps and source light curves are available through DR1 for its epoch. | Use DR2 for a deeper historical counterpart search. Its cumulative flux can dilute a short flare. DR1 and DR2 share the first scan, so their measurements are statistically dependent. [DR2 scope and dates](https://erosita.mpe.mpg.de/dr2/AllSkySurveyData_dr2/) |
| eROSITA positional upper limits | Band-specific aperture photometry with exposure, flux limits and coverage metadata. DR1 offers five energy bands and DR2 two. DR1 upper-limit FITS tables can also be downloaded per tile. | Check the footprint and exposure first. In DR1, `UL_B` uses the source-free background. Near a catalogued detection, `UL_S` includes that source’s PSF model in the background and constrains a hypothetical additional source. [Upper-limit definitions](https://erosita.mpe.mpg.de/dr1/AllSkySurveyData_dr1/UpperLimitServer_dr1/) |

For a candidate report, retain the catalogue/release or observation ID, separation, observation dates, energy band, flux or count-rate units, uncertainty or limit confidence, exposure and spectral assumptions. Several objects can fall within one EP localization. An archive match provides evidence for follow-up; a flare or AGN classification needs source identification and compatible brightness or spectral measurements.
